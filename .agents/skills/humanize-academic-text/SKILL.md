# Humanized Academic Writing & Engineering Report Guidelines

## 1. Core Principles of Authentic Student Engineering Writing

1. **High Burstiness & Variable Sentence Rhythm:**
   - Alternate between short, direct observations (5–10 words) and detailed descriptive technical sentences (25–35 words).
   - Real human students write with natural conversational cadence rather than uniform clauses.
   - Example: *"In our first test, the memory usage spiked significantly. This happened because the vector database was indexing all raw files without batching. Once we added chunk filtering, memory stabilized around 463 MB."*

2. **Grounding in Practical Implementation Realities:**
   - Detail real obstacles encountered during development:
     * Windows subprocess character encoding quirks (e.g., `charmap` crashes vs UTF-8 pipes).
     * Why NetworkX MultiDiGraph was picked over recursive SQL joins for $k$-hop entity search.
     * How prompt injection defense was tested and why safety confirmation gates were needed for destructive actions.
     * How the ReAct loop was constrained with step budgets to stop 7B models from looping infinitely.

3. **Elimination of Robotic AI Fingerprints:**
   - **Strictly Avoid AI Clichés:** *"delve into"*, *"testament to"*, *"pivotal"*, *"beacon of innovation"*, *"seamlessly orchestrates"*, *"multifaceted tapestry"*, *"in conclusion it is crucial to note that"*, *"serves as a testament"*, *"game-changer"*.
   - **Authentic Student Phrasing:** *"In our implementation"*, *"During testing on our machine"*, *"We noticed that"*, *"To solve this problem, we..."*, *"As shown in Figure 5.7 below"*, *"Coming to the memory component"*, *"In practical scenarios"*.

4. **Authentic Mumbai University Capstone Tone:**
   - Maintain clear formal structure (Chapters 1 to 8, Project Mentor Prof. Geocey Shejy, Group 19, VESIT Computer Engineering) while writing in a direct, natural student voice that examiners love reading during project vivas.
